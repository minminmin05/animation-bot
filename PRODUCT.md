# Product

## Register

product

## Users

School administrators are the primary users, managing enrollment, classes, and grading as their core workflow. Teachers, students, and parents also interact with the system daily - viewing grades, submitting assignments, checking attendance, and monitoring progress.

Users interact across devices: school computer labs, personal laptops, tablets, and phones. Contexts vary from focused admin sessions at a desk to quick grade checks on mobile between classes.

## Product Purpose

Lumaid is a school management platform that streamlines academic administration. It replaces fragmented processes (spreadsheets, paper records, disconnected tools) with a unified system for:

- Student enrollment and class management
- Grade tracking and report cards
- Attendance monitoring
- Assignment management
- Parent-teacher communication

Success is when administrative overhead fades into the background, letting schools focus on education instead of paperwork.

## Brand Personality

**Modern & Friendly** - Approachable, delightful, contemporary.

Three words: **Confident. Warm. Clear.**

The interface should feel like a thoughtful partner in education, not a bureaucratic hurdle. Reference the care of Duolingo's encouragement and the polished efficiency of Notion. Users should feel supported, not managed.

## Anti-references

- **Boring Enterprise:** Stiff data grids, generic navy blues, no personality, looks like software from 2005
- **SaaS Cliché:** Purple gradients everywhere, cartoonish 3D illustrations, overdone glassmorphism that hurts readability
- **Canvas/Blackboard:** Dated interfaces, cluttered dashboards, confusing navigation patterns

## Design Principles

1. **Confidence through clarity** - Complex data (grades, schedules, attendance) should be instantly scannable. Don't make users hunt for what matters.

2. **Warmth in micro-interactions** - Small moments of delight: animations on success, encouraging copy, responsive feedback. Institutional software can still feel human.

3. **Respect the user's attention** - Administrative tasks are necessary but not exciting. Design for efficiency first. Get out of the way once the job is done.

4. **Context-appropriate depth** - Admins need power tools; students need clarity. Surface complexity by role and task. Never overwhelm with options that don't apply.

5. **Modern without being trendy** - Build for longevity, not chase-of-the-month. Classic layouts elevated with thoughtful details, not flash that ages poorly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA compliance baseline
- Support reduced motion preferences
- Ensure color contrast ratios meet or exceed standards
- Design for color blindness (never rely on color alone to convey meaning)
- Consider users with varying technical literacy - clear labels, helpful empty states, forgiving error messages
- Mobile-responsive: administrators may use tablets, parents/students often use phones
